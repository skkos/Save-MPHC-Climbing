The full story

Everything below is what we know, with the one thing we don't know marked as such.

What's being lost

1992
MPHC's climbing gym opened, making it the oldest continuously operating climbing gym in New York City. That makes it older than Chelsea Piers and older than every gym anyone climbs at today.
$49
The monthly membership rate. Most climbing gyms in New York are $100 or more (roughly double!) and some closer to triple. Women climb free every Friday, which exists nowhere else in Manhattan. This is a critical driver of accessibility in a male-dominated sport.
90+
Number of young climbers on the wall every week. Two high schools run after-school climbing programmes there, thirty students each, and there's a summer camp for children who live in the building. On October 1 they have nowhere to go.
155
Climbing members plus 10 staff who lose their jobs..

What's happening

Sept 30
The day it closes permanently. Members and staff were told by email with less than a month's notice. No reason has been given publicly, to anyone, as far as we can tell.
Reportedly,
a tennis
simulator
What members have heard is replacing it, in a complex that already has five rooftop tennis courts. Unconfirmed by the club or its owner. We've asked, and we'd genuinely like to be wrong.

Who decides

Related
Companies
Owns Manhattan Plaza, the 1,689-apartment complex the gym sits inside, built with public support to house working artists. The health club that the climbing gym is a part of is one of its amenities.

We want MPHC to stay true to its own stated mission: MPHC's own About page: the club "has built its reputation on being an affordable place in the community where people from all walks of life can go to maintain their health & wellness." A $49 climbing gym with free Friday sessions, two school programmes and a summer camp for the kids upstairs is an affordable place for people from all walks of life. We're not asking Related to become something new. We're asking them to keep the part of the building that already does what they say it does.

What we're asking for

Three options.

1

Keep the climbing gym open.

Best outcome. If the problem is that the wall doesn't earn enough, the price hasn't been revisited in years — there's room to fix that without closing anything.

2

Or lease the space to another climbing operator.

The wall, the anchors, the holds and the members are already here. New York has several gym companies. Let one of them run it — Related collects rent instead of running a gym.

3

Give it 90 days and tell people why.

Push the date to December 31 so a handover can actually be explored. This costs almost nothing to grant.

New York's oldest climbing gym is closing.

The climbing community is losing a historic fixture and a truly rare gem. MPHC in Hell's Kitchen has been open since 1992 - it's the oldest climbing gym in New York and the last affordable climbing in Manhattan. It closes September 30. No reason has been given.

We're asking the owner to keep it open or at least delay and tell us why. Your voice is the only thing that can save it.
